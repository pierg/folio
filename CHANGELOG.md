# Changelog

## Unreleased

A paper is written for readers outside the library.

- `\fcite` prints nothing in the built PDF. The ids stay in the LaTeX source, where the gate and the lab pack's `uncited-number` rule read them, and the sentence around each one must stand on its own. `folio paper build <slug> --ids` makes a review copy that prints each id after the text it backs, raised and in brackets, linked to its page at the charter's `site_url`; `folio paper freeze` always builds the plain copy. `folio.sty` takes the same as a package option, `\usepackage[ids]{folio}`.
- The paper genre's card says who the reader is (someone who has never seen the library), what the reader gets (a page that stands on its own, each section opening with its finding, counts in prose and intervals in tables, the counting rules stated once in the method) and what is forbidden (a library id or term the reader can see, a results section that reads as an audit trail). The write and publish skills, the lab pack and the specs say the same.
- The abstract `folio index` reads from `main.tex` drops each `\fcite` as the PDF does, instead of printing its id.
- The paper skeleton's fallback, for a build without `folio.sty`, is `\providecommand{\fcite}[1]{}`.

Migrating: in every paper, move each `\fcite` so that its sentence reads whole without it (`7 of 9\fcite{R-12}.`, not `7 of 9 (\fcite{R-12}).`), and take every id, result page and lab term out of the prose, tables and figures. `folio paper build --ids` shows where each id stands while you revise.

## 0.3.0 (2026-10-20)

Every page has one place: one row on one home map, under one heading. Sources are cited, not placed. Pages can land live and be reviewed after.

- The genre card's `on_map` takes two new values. `placed`: the document is exactly one row on one of the charter's home maps, under a heading of that map. `cited`: it is never a map row; the maps whose pages cite it list it. Concept, note, entry, guide, deck, reading and survey are `placed`; source is `cited`. `required` and `optional` still work, for the other genres and for a library's own cards. For the no-orphans rule, `placed` and `cited` are met as `optional` is.
- A new gate check, `placement` (warning; the charter makes it an error), names a placed page on no home map, on two or more, or outside every heading (rows above a map's first heading are under none), with the maps, and a cited page that is a row. A link in a map's prose is not a place, and neither is a row on a map that is not a home map. A second new check, `heading-size` (warning), names a map heading holding more than 12 rows.
- `folio index` writes each placed document's `place: {map, section, anchor}` into `catalog.json` (`null` while it has no single place), and each cited document's `used_by`: the home maps that link it or whose rows cite it.
- The shell draws a path above the title: `Home › Map › Heading › Page`, the heading linking to its anchor on the map; a source shows `Home › Sources › Page` and the maps it is used in. The rail's map groups read the same `place`, so a placed page sits only in its own map, and a draft shows there with a draft mark instead of being hidden. A map page's panel lists its Sources: every source its rows cite, grouped by heading, with counts.
- `folio map add` takes `--section <heading>` (`--group` still works). While `placement` is not off, it refuses a placed page that already has a place on another home map, a placed row on a home map outside every heading, and a cited page on any map. `folio map move <doc> --to <map>[#<heading>] [--from <map>]` moves a row in one step, keeping its reason.
- `folio new` says when a new page still needs its place.
- Review after landing: `save.review: after`. `folio new` starts a page live and puts it on the To review list; `folio save` puts on it every page it records for the first time; `folio review add <doc> --note ".."` puts back a substantively revised one. Each mark lives in the page's annotation file, under `review`, and the server commits it like a comment. The Review page has a To review tab, newest first, with a Reviewed button; `folio review list [--all]` and `folio review done <doc>` do the same from the command line. The home page's line counts the pages to review. `drafts` stays available.
- The write skill makes choosing the one home map and heading a required step, lands pages live under `after`, and after adding pages runs organise's new rebalance pass on the maps it touched: new pages are placed without asking, and every move or rename of an existing page, heading or map is proposed in one short table and waits for the owner's yes, or is left as a question on the map page. The organise skill moves rows with `folio map move`.

Migrating a library to 0.3.0:

1. Run `folio check`: the `placement` warnings name every page with no single place. Give each one home with `folio map move <doc> --to <map>#<heading>`, and remove the extra rows with `folio map rm`; link the page from the other maps' prose where it helps. Remove the rows of sources.
2. Turn the check on: `folio config set checks.placement error`.
3. To land pages live and review them after, `folio config set save.review after`, then put each remaining draft on the list as it goes live: `folio review add <doc> --note ".."`.
4. `folio skills update`, then commit `.agents/skills/`.

## 0.2.3 (2026-10-19)

- A library can keep its evidence apart in the rail. The charter's `rail: { evidence: [protocol, result] }` names genres that are audit records rather than reading: the rail's "By genre" section then lists the other genres in reading order, and the evidence genres follow in one closed group headed Evidence, which counts them while closed and opens, as any group does, on the page being read. Without the option the packs decide: a pack's `PACK.md` may name its own genres under `evidence`, and the lab pack names `protocol` and `result`, so a lab library gets this without a change to its charter. `rail: { evidence: [] }` lists every genre together in "By genre", by name, as before; an empty `evidence:` is an error asking for `[]`. `folio config get|set rail.evidence` reads and sets it, and the new gate check `rail-genres` (error) names a genre in it that does not exist or is switched off.
- The rail's reading order places the lab genres: guide, entry, report, concept, claim, question, note, survey, reading, source, map, protocol, result, then any other genre by name. A long map without sections lists its genres in this order.

## 0.2.1 (2026-10-15)

- Code listings are coloured by their language. The shell vendors highlight.js 11.11.1 (its common languages, plus Verilog and SystemVerilog, VHDL, Tcl, Haskell, OCaml, Coq, Lisp, Scheme, LaTeX, Dockerfile and Nix, and a small Lean grammar) and loads it only on a page with a `<pre><code>`. A `language-<name>` class on the code, the `<pre>` or an element around them names the language, as a Markdown fence does; without one the shell guesses, gives a short or unclear listing the page's main language when it fits, and leaves the rest, such as program output, plain. `language-plaintext` or `nohighlight` opts out, and a listing with markup inside is never touched. The colours come from the shell's registers, so they follow the theme. `craft/code.md` drops its rule against highlighting and asks for the language class.

## 0.2.0 (2026-10-14)

Pages get a free canvas again. A genre gives structure and voice; the form is the page's. Content is saved in one step, and a served page follows its own file.

- `folio save` records a library's content: its documents and annotation files under `content/`, its assets and `.folio/`. It pulls with rebase, regenerates the indices (rebuilt, never merged), runs the gate, commits only those folders and pushes, taking its commit back and trying again when the branch moved. It refuses, saving nothing, when the gate fails or a pull meets the same lines changed upstream, prints each saved document with its status and live address, and names the changed files it left alone because they are not content. `--no-push` commits only. The write, address, organise and run skills save with it.
- The charter's `save:` block: `gate` (the command run before saving; default `folio check`), `review: drafts` (a new document starts as `draft`, a substantive edit to a `live` one goes back to `draft`, and only the owner makes a document `live`) and `url` (where the library is served live).
- A page served by `folio serve` follows its own file: every few seconds, while in view, it asks `/_folio/etag` for its current ETag and reloads when it changed; while the reader is typing it offers a reload instead.
- A ninth core genre, `deck`, carries a talk: `<div class="deck">` holds a `<section class="slide">` per slide, each drawn at 1600 by 900 and scaled to its frame, with the speaker's notes in `<aside class="notes">`. The page shows every slide complete with its notes, for reading and comments; Present (P) shows one slide at a time full screen and steps through its builds (`data-step`, `data-until`); S opens a presenter's window with the next slide, the notes and a timer, kept in step over a `BroadcastChannel`; PDF downloads the slides as the page draws them, one per page: `folio serve` renders it with headless Chrome on request (`/_folio/pdf?doc=<deck>`), and `folio export` renders each deck once and publishes `<id>.pdf` beside it, listed in `site.json` as `pdfs`; without Chrome (or `$FOLIO_CHROME`) the server answers with the reason and the export warns. `data-theme` on the deck fixes its theme. The card requires `deck` and `slide` and a citation. A slide's notes may hold talking points (`ul.points`) beside a script (`div.script`); H hides the scripts on the page and in the presenter's window, to rehearse from the points. A point or script paragraph with `data-step="n"` belongs to build n: stepping a slide, on the page (its arrows, or a click on it) or in the presenter's window, lights the current build's lines and fades the later ones.
- A comment's quote may run across tags. A selection such as `always</b>, and the first` was refused ("the quote is not on"), because the page's extracted text puts a space after every closing tag; the server and the stale-quote check now compare a quote with whitespace ignored, and the page highlights a quote that spans several elements, one mark per piece.
- A thread's author can delete it from the served page: the comment panel shows a Delete button on the reader's own threads, asks for a confirmation, and the server commits the deletion ("Delete a comment on <id>"), so git keeps what was said. The thread's id moves to the annotation file's new `deleted` list and is never reused.
- A comment thread opened from its highlighted passage no longer lays its parts out side by side: it used the class `f-focus`, which the top bar's Focus control also uses; it is now `f-thread-on`.
- Every page's header has a button showing the page's path in the library (`content/entries/x/` for a folder page, the file otherwise); clicking it copies the path, to hand the page to an agent or a command.
- A page stands alone: it states what a source says, never where in the source. The knowledge-base pack adds the rule check `source-pointer` (warning), which flags a section, table, figure, listing, footnote, equation or appendix number in a page's prose (`Section 5.3`, `Table 7`, `§4`, `Appendix C`), skipping code, attributes, permanent and locked documents, and labels the page or its guide defines itself. The reading card, its skeleton and the write skill (rule 24) say to state the claim, redraw a figure that matters, explain a coined name, and quote without a location.
- The shell's icons keep their size inside a page's figure: a figure rule such as `.xx-fig svg { min-width: 40rem }` no longer stretches the expand button's icon and pushes the page wide on a phone. `craft/layout.md` says to size the figure's own SVG (`.xx-scroll > svg`), not every `svg` in the figure.
- A library links documents in other libraries on the same machine. The charter names them under `libraries:`, each with its `path` and the `url` where it is served, and a link names the library as its scheme: `<a href="pier-folio:/content/concepts/cti/">`. The gate checks it against that library (`broken-link`), and warns once per library that is not at its path (`library-link`). `folio serve` and `folio export` send it to the library's `url`, marked `data-library`, and the shell names the library after the link. `folio rm <doc> --to <library>:<id>` retires a document into another library: links lead there, a map drops its row, and the address redirects there. `folio cite <library>:<id>` prints the link, and `folio search --all` searches these libraries too.
- Every HTML page is a canvas: all the room between the rail and the page panel, with its own `<style>` and `<script>`, adapting with `@container folio-canvas` queries. The reading column is opt-in with `<meta name="layout" content="column">`; Markdown records always use it. The gate checks `layout` is `canvas` or `column`.
- Genre cards check substance, not form. Word caps are gone from every page genre (they stay on journal entries, results and claims). Gone too: `forbid: [h2]` on concept and note, the `require` parts nothing reads (entry `thesis` and `h2`, project `state` and `next`, guide `start` and chapter sections, reading, source `about`, survey and report sections), and the `check_each_section` check. `forward_links` is judged unless a library sets it. `min_sources` counts the sources a survey cites anywhere. Each card's Shape is now guidance, judged, not checked.
- The shell adds the colour registers (`--teal`, `--indigo`, `--blue`, `--amber`, `--red`; `--azure`, `--violet`, `--orange`; `--kept`, `--discarded`, `--rejected`, `--untested`), a few shared components (`.cols`, `.lane`, `.note`, `.law`, `.chip`, `.v`, `.tbtn`) and KaTeX math, vendored, on a page with `<meta name="math">`.
- `craft/layout.md` holds the canvas contract and the "choosing a form" playbook; new `code.md`, `comparison.md` and `timeline.md` guides. The write skill designs the page, and `folio genre <name>` ends with where the craft guides are installed.
- The documentation library is rebuilt as canvas pages.
- A document is judged by its content, never its size. Five questions, judged, not checked, apply to every genre: does it earn a page (not trivial, durable, adding something), is this its one home, is it clear, is it complete and nothing more, does the form serve the content (model.md §3, the write skill's rule 23). Each card gains `## Earns its page`, with examples rather than thresholds. A concept no longer needs two documents linking it, and a well-known part of a larger system can be one; an implementation detail of one system as built stays in its document. The write skill asks the questions before it writes, and no longer makes a concept of every term explained twice.
- The concept card asks for the field's name for a term. A name the library coins earns a concept only when it is clear, does not read as a better-known meaning elsewhere, and is flagged as the library's; a taxonomy the library made up lives in the entry that frames the topic. The organise audit asks this of every definition document.
- A link clicked in the rail stays under the pointer on the page it opens, however the rail's groups open or close there.
- A flag marks only what needs the owner's judgement: an unsupported claim the owner may know better, an unverified number or name, a passage the agent could not settle. The agent's framing, layout, wording, illustrative examples, the library's synthesis and corrections of plain errors raise none. The write skill, the spec and the cards say so; an illustrative example says so in the page instead.
- The shell shows an inline SVG near the size its `viewBox` was drawn for, so labels no longer grow on a wide canvas (`data-fit="wide"` opts out), and any figure opens larger in a dialog from a button in its corner, with its controls still working; on a phone the dialog shows a wide drawing at its drawn size and scrolls sideways, so its labels can be read. `craft/figures.md` gains a checklist for drawing an inline figure.
- A retired document is held only to its lifecycle checks, so a retired survey no longer warns that it cites too few sources.
- A link to a page's `#section` lands on the section again once the shell has drawn the page.
- `craft/layout.md` adds restraint (a figure, control or animation earns its place by showing what prose cannot) and says length follows the content. The concept skeleton drops its fixed "Why it matters / The trap / Related" sections.
- The organise skill audits a library against the current cards: a verdict per document, a report to the owner, then batches the owner approves. `folio skills [update]` compares a library's copy of the skills with the engine's and refreshes the stale ones.
- The rail keeps each map's own sections: `folio index` records, for a map, the heading each list of rows sits under (`sections` in `catalog.json`, beside the flat `rows`), and the rail shows them as sub-groups that count their genres while closed. A map without sections lists a long run of documents by genre, in reading order. Every document in a map's group carries its genre's mark, with a key at the foot of the rail, and the map's first document is marked Start. The rail is a little wider.
- `folio serve` loads the library once and again only when a file, the shell or the last commit changes; it used to load it on every request. Pages and files carry an ETag and are answered with 304 when unchanged.
- The shell keeps a page hidden until it has drawn the top bar, rail and panel, and set any math, so no page shows half drawn; it shows anyway after 1.5 s should the script fail, and at once without scripting. KaTeX and its common fonts load beside the indices instead of after them. Where the browser supports speculation rules, a linked page is prerendered while the pointer rests on its link.

## 0.1.0 (2026-10-04)

The first release.

- The `folio` command: create a library, write documents from genre skeletons, keep maps, the journal and comment threads, move and retire documents with links kept, search, serve and export a static site, and build and freeze papers.
- The gate, `folio check`: links, genre cards, fields, placeholders, lifecycles checked against git, orphans, unique ids, current indices and stale quotes, with severities set in the charter.
- Eight core genres: concept, note, entry, map, guide, project, journal, paper.
- Two packs: knowledge-base (source, reading, survey; ingest and quiz) and lab (question, protocol, result, claim, report; record-a-result and write-a-report).
- Seven skills: set-up, configure, write, organise, address, publish, run.
- The shell: one stylesheet and one script for every page, in light and dark, with the library rail and a focus on one or more topics.
- The documentation in `docs/`, itself a folio library, and the specification in `docs/spec/`.
