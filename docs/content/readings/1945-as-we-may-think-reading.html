What it claims

  • The record of what people know has outgrown the means of consulting it (the essay's opening argument).
  • A device the essay names the memex, a desk that stores a person's books, records and letters, could hold everything that person consults and show any of it quickly (where the memex is introduced).
  • The memex would join items by association, as the mind does, rather than file each one in a single place: any item can call up another, and a run of such joins forms a trail that is kept and can be followed again (where trails are described).
  • A trail can be copied and handed to someone else, who adds it to their own memex (the same passage).

My take

The trail is the part that has aged best. A link joins two items; a trail is an ordered path through many, made by someone for a reason, and handed on so a reader can follow the same path. That is close to what a map is in a folio library: rows in reading order, each with a reason to follow it.

What the essay leaves to the person, folio leaves to a gate. A memex trail can point at an item that was later changed or lost, and nothing notices. In a library, a link that no longer resolves fails the gate, and a record that was wrong is corrected by a new one that points back, as How corrections work explains.
