Maps are the structure

A library's folders follow genres, so they do not tell a reader where to start. Maps do. Each map lists documents in the order to read them, under group headings, with an optional reason per row. The maps named in the charter as home maps appear on the home page as the library's topics.

Every document except a journal entry must be on a map, or linked from a document that is. The gate reports any that are not: an orphan is a page no reader can reach by following the library from its home page.

A genre can ask for more. A guide or an entry needs a row of its own on some map; a link to it is not enough. A concept or a note is reachable either way.

A page is on no map, but a page on a map links to it. Is it an orphan?
No. A link from a document that is on a map makes it reachable, unless its genre requires a row of its own.

Ask for the change, not the commands

Say "tidy the maps", "rename this page", "merge these two", "this note grew, make it an entry", or "retire this into that". The agent follows the organise skill. It moves documents only through folio's own commands, so links are rewritten, comments and dates are kept, and a committed document keeps its id while its old address redirects to the new one.

Follow one rename through. You ask: "rename the pinned-parser note to parser-pin". Watch the map row that lists it.

Why should the agent never move a page with a plain file move?
A plain move breaks every link to the page and loses its comments. folio's move rewrites the links and keeps the comments, the id and the dates.

Ask for a health pass

From time to time, ask "is the library healthy?". The agent runs the gate, lists documents on no map, looks for thin documents and duplicates, finds drafts left for weeks and open comments, and reports what it changed and what is left for you. Structural choices, such as a new topic or retiring something you wrote, stay yours.

The agent checks and fixes

  1. The gate passes.
  2. Documents on no map get a row, or a link from one.
  3. Thin documents fold into the one they belong to.
  4. Two homes for one fact become one.
  5. Drafts left for weeks are finished, or proposed for retirement.
  6. Open comments go to the address skill.

It asks you first

  1. A new topic on the home page.
  2. Retiring or merging away something you wrote.
  3. Two documents that disagree on a fact: which holds.

Each reorganisation is recorded in the journal, so later you can see when a page moved and why.

Who decides to add a new top-level map?
You do. It is a change to the charter, made through the configure skill and recorded as a decision.
