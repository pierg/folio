Permanent record
A permanent record is a document whose genre declares it is never edited once committed, such as a journal entry or a result. It writes no status of its own; its status is derived from the documents that point back to it.

A citation of a permanent record always shows what was cited, because the record cannot change under it. The gate compares each one with its first commit, so the promise is checked, not hoped for.

Permanent, frozen and revised

Permanent

Never edited once committed. A journal entry and a result. Its status is derived, never written.

Frozen

Edited freely until it reaches a state such as locked, then never again, except a status change to one of its card's exits. A protocol and a report.

Revised

Edited in place, and always current. Concepts, entries, maps and the rest of the pages.

The trap: thinking permanent means it can never be wrong. It can be wrong, and the library says so: a newer record supersedes it, or a journal entry retracts or corrects it, and a banner on the record links the correction. What never happens is a quiet edit.
