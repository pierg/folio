Genre
A genre is a kind of document, such as a concept, a note or a result. It fixes the document's job, its reader, its voice, its format, where it lives, which metadata it carries, how it may change over time, and which checks it must pass.

An agent writing a document reads its genre's card first, every time. So two documents of one genre read as if one author wrote them, and the gate can check what each genre promises. Voice is never set per document.

A genre gives structure and voice, never form. How an HTML page looks is its author's, on a free canvas: the card fixes what the page is, and the page decides how to show it.

Where a genre comes from

A genre is a folder holding its card and a skeleton. folio looks for it in three places, and the first one found wins. An override in the library holds only what it changes, so the card underneath still applies and folio's upgrades flow in.

  1. First

    The library's own genres/<name>/: an override, a variant or a genre of its own.

  2. Then

    A pack switched on in the charter, which adds genres under names no other genre uses.

  3. Last

    folio's core: concept, note, entry, map, guide, project, journal, paper and deck.

A variant, for a second audience

A genre can extend another. The variant inherits everything and overrides only what it states: usually the voice, the reader and the limits. A paper for a workshop extends the paper. A document changes genre only by promotion, as when a note that grew sections becomes an entry, and promotion keeps its id, links, comments and dates.

The trap: treating a genre as a template. The skeleton is the smallest part of it. The card also says what the document is for and what it must never do, and most of that is judged by the agent, not checked.

Related

The core has nine genres. A pack adds more, and a library can override a genre or add a variant for a second audience.

Where it is checked

The gate runs each card's checks on every document of its genre. Whether a page does its job well is left to the agent and the owner.
